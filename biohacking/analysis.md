# Current Analysis

This file is an evolving interpretation of `log.md`, not a source of historical facts.

## Current question

Does morning caffeine contribute to severe afternoon fatigue between 14:00 and 16:00?

## Prediction

If morning caffeine is an important contributor, severe afternoon fatigue should occur more often after caffeine than on reasonably comparable caffeine-free days.

## Evidence so far

- On 2026-09-10, severe afternoon lethargy occurred in a routine that included sweetened morning coffee, a rice-heavy lunch, and afternoon sweets.
- By 2026-09-14, no severe lethargy was noticed on caffeine-free days, although mild fatigue remained.
- The evidence is weak because caffeine, added sugar, fruit intake, meal sequencing, walking, and snack choices were not held constant.

## Alternative explanations

- Added sugar or overall meal composition
- Sleep duration or quality, which was not recorded
- Normal circadian variation
- Cognitive workload or stress
- Lunch size, nap duration, or exercise
- Ordinary day-to-day variation

## Smallest useful next test

On otherwise typical workdays, record whether caffeine was consumed, approximate sleep duration, and the worst fatigue between 14:00 and 16:00. Gather at least one caffeine day and one caffeine-free day before adding another deliberate intervention.

## Confidence

Low. The current observations are compatible with a caffeine effect but do not isolate it.
